What was explored

A PointNet-style encoder turns an unordered sparse surface representation into geometry features for the actor. A previously trained rock-pickup policy supplies imitation examples, followed by PPO fine-tuning across the full object catalog.

20 surface points + robot state
→ PointNet encoder + actor MLP
→ arm and finger commands

The grapple has two passive suspension hinges. The policy commands the arm and fingers rather than directly actuating those hinges.

What counts as success?

  • Lift the object 2 m above its initial center-of-mass height.
  • Maintain a stable grasp continuously for 2 seconds, with ground clearance and low motion/slip.
  • Keep the carrier within 15° of vertical and the passive hinges inside their configured bounds.

Reward and hold-criterion revisions addressed a learned strategy that used suspension stops for support. Supported-carry tilt averaged 4.4°, with nominal stop contact for about 0.9% of that time.

Current scope and limitations

These are simulation results on the training asset catalog with new reset cases. Sparse points and state feedback currently come from the simulator, rather than cameras or LiDAR. This is not a real-hardware demonstration or an unseen-shape generalization claim. Thin sticks, some large irregular objects, drops and settling remain challenges. This repository shares videos and a written project description; the private simulator, training code, assets and policy weights are not distributed.
